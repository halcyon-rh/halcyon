How it works

Three steps, one transaction.

01

Pick a position

Choose an asset, a strike price and an expiry date. Calls lock the asset; puts lock stablecoins. Both stay in an escrow tied to your wallet.

02

Desks compete

Your request goes out over a live quote channel. Market makers answer within seconds and the highest bid rises to the top.

03

Settle and earn

Accept a bid. The option is minted and the premium lands in your wallet in the same block. No waiting, no trusting a middleman.

An example

You hold 2 ETH.
Here's what one trade looks like.

ETH trades at $2,350 right now. You write a 14-day covered call at a $2,550 strike, and a desk pays you $40 upfront. Two weeks later, one of two things happens.

ETH stays below $2,550

The option expires. You keep your 2 ETH and the $40. Write another one if you like.

ETH rises above $2,550

Your ETH is sold at $2,550 each. You keep the $40 plus the gain up to the strike, but miss anything above it.

Uses the live ETH price; premium is a model estimate. Options carry risk, and premium is never guaranteed.

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

What is a covered call?

You agree to sell an asset you own at a set price (the strike) if it rises above that price before a set date. In return, a buyer pays you a fee, called premium, right away.

What is a cash-secured put?

You agree to buy an asset at a set price if it falls below that price. You lock the stablecoins needed to buy it, and you get paid premium upfront for making that promise.

Who pays the premium?

Professional market makers. They bid against each other for your trade, so you see several prices and pick the best one.

Can I lose money?

Yes. With a call, you give up gains above the strike. With a put, you may end up buying an asset that keeps falling. Premium softens this, but doesn't remove it.
